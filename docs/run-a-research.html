Nota Recognition Stack

What the literature says about recognising handwritten Indonesian purchase notes from 440 receipts and three writers, on a CPU — and the one finding that reorders the whole plan.

SNAPTOCK AI16 Aug 202612 verified sourcesRun B (forecasting) pending

The recommendation

Fine-tune a CTC-based PP-OCR recognizer, not a transformer seq2seq model and not a document VLM. Attack textual domain gap before visual domain gap: mine Indonesian product vocabulary and price distributions from CORD, render it as synthetic handwriting with a diffusion HTG model, and composite into the real nota template. Assign fields by column geometry, not a learned model. Use the arithmetic identity as the correction oracle that a CTC decoder's missing language model would otherwise provide.

Every clause above is a decision the evidence forced, and each is defended below with the trade-off it accepts.

The finding that reorders the plan

Textual divergence hurts generalization more than visual divergence

The largest controlled study of HTR generalization to date analysed 336 out-of-distribution cases across eight state-of-the-art HTR models, seven datasets and five languages. Its headline conclusion: “the most significant factor for generalization lies in the textual divergence between domains, followed by visual divergence.” [1]

I previously told you handwriting-style diversity was the critical path. On this evidence that ordering is wrong. Your corpus has a 739-word closed vocabulary — when a supplier writes a product you have never seen, that is textual divergence, and it is the failure mode that will dominate. Style diversity still matters; it matters second.

Why it changes the buildThe synthetic data pipeline should be driven first by vocabulary breadth — tens of thousands of real Indonesian product names and plausible prices — and only second by handwriting-style variety. Those are different generators with different priorities, and you would have built them in the wrong order.

CORD gives you exactly that vocabulary, in Indonesian

CORD (Consolidated Receipt Dataset) is 11,000 Indonesian receipts from shops and restaurants, annotated with 30 fine-grained semantic entities including menu name, quantity, unit price and tax; the public release is 800/100/100 train/val/test. [2] SROIE adds 1,000 scanned receipts with company, date, address and total. [3]

CORD's receipts are printed, so it is nearly useless as handwriting training data. That is not what you need it for. You need its text: real Indonesian product names, real price magnitudes, real quantity conventions — precisely the axis finding [1] identifies as dominant. Treat CORD as a language resource, not an image resource.

Trade-off acceptedCORD is restaurant and shop receipts, not distributor notes. Its vocabulary skews toward prepared food and retail goods. Expect partial coverage of a hardware or wholesale supplier's catalogue.

Synthetic handwriting works, and the published gains are large at your data scale

A 2025 ICCV workshop analysis of handwritten text generation as HTR augmentation reports CER reductions of 13.3% to 35.7%, and a setting where a transformer recognizer trained on 5,000 real handwriting images improved from 25.50% CER to 3.13% once 25,000 synthetic images were added. [4]

Your usable real signal — roughly 6,300 handwritten tokens — sits in the same order of magnitude as that 5,000-image baseline, which is the strongest reason to believe synthetic generation is worth building properly rather than bolting on.

For the generator itself, latent-diffusion HTG is the current line of work: DiffusionPen conditions style from as few as five exemplars using a hybrid metric-learning and classification style extractor [5]; WordStylist established styled verbatim generation with latent diffusion [6]; more recent entries include semi-supervised diffusion adaptation [7] and ViT-based generation [8].

CautionThese CER figures come from the source paper's own experimental settings on standard benchmarks, not from Indonesian handwritten forms. Treat the direction as well-supported and the magnitude as an upper bound to verify on your own data, not a number to quote in a proposal.

Architecture: why CTC beats the alternatives here

Three families were considered. The constraint that decides it is not accuracy in the abstract — it is accuracy achievable from ~6,300 real tokens, running on a judge's CPU.

The strongest single data point against the VLM route is that it now loses on its own ground. PP-OCRv6 reports a 34.5M-parameter model outperforming Qwen3-VL-235B, GPT-5.5 and Gemini-3.1-Pro on OCR tasks, with a tiny tier running 3.9× faster than PP-OCRv5-mobile on Intel Xeon CPU, and explicitly motivates the work by VLMs' “hallucination, imprecise localization, and prohibitive computational cost” in dedicated OCR settings. [12]

Two honest caveats on that headline

Those comparisons are run on the authors' in-house benchmarks — a vendor evaluating its own model against competitors, not an independent benchmark. Discount accordingly.

And PP-OCRv6's abstract does not mention handwriting at all, whereas PP-OCRv5 documents specific handwriting recognition improvements over v4. [13] For a handwritten-input product, start from v5, benchmark v6, and let your own numbers decide — do not assume the newer version is better on your domain.

The trade-off you are accepting: CTC decodes largely position-by-position and lacks the language-model context that makes seq2seq models strong on ambiguous handwriting. That is a real cost. The next section is how you pay it back.

The reconciliation layer, honestly assessed

You asked whether the arithmetic self-correction idea is novel. One search pass cannot prove a negative, but the picture is clear enough to keep you out of trouble.

  • Arithmetic validation of invoices is standard commercial practice. Recalculating quantity × unit price against line totals, and line totals against the invoice sum, is routine in accounts-payable OCR products. As a product feature it is not novel.
  • Confidence-driven candidate search is established post-OCR literature. The ACM Computing Surveys review of post-OCR processing documents systems combining a character language model, an error model and a hypothesis model built from character recognition confidences, compiled into weighted finite-state transducers and decoded by lowest-cost path, plus confusion-matrix candidate generation. [14] The mechanism you proposed already has a literature.
  • What is genuinely under-served is using the arithmetic identity itself as the correction oracle — substituting a document-internal mathematical constraint for the language model in that established pipeline — and doing it on handwritten Indonesian nota. A 2026 applied paper explicitly frames arithmetic validation of line-item totals as a gap that the extraction-focused literature ignores, though it appears in a low-tier venue and should carry little evidential weight. [15]

How to claim it: as an engineering contribution and an empirical result on a new domain — “we substitute a document-internal arithmetic constraint for the language model in the standard confidence-driven post-OCR correction framework, and measure the repair rate on handwritten Indonesian nota” — not as a novel method. That version is true, defensible under questioning, and still differentiating. Your audit already gives you the number that makes it credible: the constraint holds on 98.1% of line items.

One design warning the literature implies. The identity q × p = j is under-determined: many digit substitutions satisfy it, and a repair that restores arithmetic while inventing a wrong price is worse than no repair. Bound the candidate search by per-character confidence, cap edit distance, and measure false-repair rate as a first-class metric, not just repair rate.

Benchmark protocol

Designed so each recommendation above can be falsified on your own data. All splits group by source receipt, never image_id; the primary split holds out writers, because per finding [1] textual and visual divergence — not receipt identity — are what break in deployment.

Risks, and where this is least certain

Three writers may be too few to seed style generation. DiffusionPen-style few-shot conditioning needs style exemplars to imitate. You have ~3 writers, which may produce three well-imitated styles rather than genuine diversity. Mitigation: seed styles from a public handwriting corpus such as IAM and transfer, rather than relying only on your own writers — but this is the weakest link in the recommended plan.

The CER gains in [4] are not from your domain. They come from standard HTR benchmarks in other languages and scripts. Indonesian handwritten forms with a fixed tabular template may behave differently in either direction.

Novelty cannot be established by one search pass. The reconciliation assessment says what I found, not what exists. Before any originality claim reaches the proposal, search specifically for prior art on constraint-driven OCR correction in tabular financial documents.

The deployment gap is unmeasured and unaddressed. Every training photograph is flat, sharp and evenly lit. Nothing in this plan yet tests angled, shadowed or creased input, and no augmentation in the corpus simulates it. Document rectification and realistic photometric augmentation were scoped into this run but are the thinnest-researched part of it.
